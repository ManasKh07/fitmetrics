How Much Water Do You Actually Need?

The "8 glasses a day" rule has almost no scientific basis — it came from a 1945 US dietary recommendation that included fluid from food, and somehow lost that context along the way. Actual needs depend on your body weight, how active you are, the climate you're in, and what you eat. Heavier people and active people need more. Hot days add to it. Fruit and vegetables contribute meaningfully too.

The 33ml/kg baseline this calculator uses is a reasonable starting estimate. For a deeper look: Daily Water Intake — The Real Answer.

FAQs

Does coffee count toward my daily water intake?

Yes. Despite caffeine's mild diuretic effect, research consistently shows that regular coffee drinkers don't end up more dehydrated — the fluid in the drink offsets the diuretic effect. Tea counts too.

How do I know if I'm drinking enough?

Look at your urine. Pale yellow (like weak lemonade) means you're well hydrated. Dark yellow or amber means drink more. Completely clear can actually mean you're overdoing it.

How much extra should I drink when exercising?

Roughly 400–600ml per hour of moderate exercise as a starting point, more in heat or for intense training. Thirst is actually a fairly reliable guide once you're not chronically dehydrated to begin with.
