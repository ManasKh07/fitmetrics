What TDEE Is and Why It Matters

TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure) is the total calories your body burns in a day — including your resting metabolism, physical activity, and the energy used just to digest food. It's the most important number in nutrition because everything else — losing fat, building muscle, maintaining weight — depends on how your intake compares to it.

This calculator uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, which research consistently shows to be the most accurate formula for estimating BMR in the general population. The result is an estimate — real TDEE can only be measured in a metabolic chamber. Use it as a starting point, track results over 2–3 weeks, and adjust. FAQs

How accurate is this estimate?

Mifflin-St Jeor is typically within 10% of true TDEE for most people. That's accurate enough to be a useful starting point, but not precise enough to follow blindly. Track your actual weight for 2–3 weeks and adjust ±100–200 kcal based on what happens.

How often should I recalculate?

Every 4–6 weeks if your weight has changed meaningfully, or if your activity level shifts. As you lose weight, your TDEE goes down — which is why weight loss often slows over time without adjusting intake.

Why is my TDEE different on other calculators?

Different formulas give slightly different results. Mifflin-St Jeor, Harris-Benedict, and Katch-McArdle all estimate BMR differently. Mifflin-St Jeor has the best validation data for the general population, which is why this calculator uses it.
